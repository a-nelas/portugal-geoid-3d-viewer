Geoid vs ellipsoid

The geoid is where gravity says “sea level” is: the shape the oceans would settle into if they could flow freely under the continents. It is lumpy because Earth’s gravity is lumpy. Mountain ranges and dense rock pull the level surface up and down.

The ellipsoid is a smooth, slightly flattened sphere defined by a formula. It ignores gravity entirely; it exists so that GPS has a simple surface to measure from. GeodPT08 uses GRS80, the ellipsoid of ETRS89, which is practically identical to WGS84.

Over mainland Portugal the geoid sits 44.9–58.2 m above the ellipsoid. That gap is N, the geoid undulation.

Three heights, one point

Pick any point P on the ground:

  • h, ellipsoidal height: the raw GNSS/GPS reading, measured from the ellipsoid.
  • H, orthometric height: measured from the geoid, i.e. height above mean sea level. This is what maps and road signs mean.
  • N: the gap between the two surfaces at that spot.

H = h − N

That subtraction is what this grid is for: it turns GPS heights into real elevations. Try it in the Pinned point panel.

Altitude vs elevation

Elevation belongs to the ground: how high a place sits above sea level (its H). A town’s elevation doesn’t change.

Altitude belongs to an object: how high something is right now, such as a plane, a drone or you on a trail. It needs a stated reference: above mean sea level (MSL) or above the ground (AGL).

A GPS receiver’s raw “altitude” is h. In Portugal it reads roughly 45–58 m too high until you subtract N.

About the data

  • GeodPT08.dat: the Portuguese geoid model from the Direção-Geral do Território (DGT), a regular grid of lon lat N rows.
  • CNTR_BN_20M_2024_4326.geojson: GISCO country boundary lines (1:20 million, WGS84), © EuroGeographics. Madeira and the Azores are left out (longitude < −12°).
